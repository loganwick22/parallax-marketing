What the first screen does

  1. Phone number and a call button in the header of every page.
  2. The headline says what they do and where they do it.
  3. One main action: call. There's no form to fill in.
  4. Licensing, experience and the cleanup promise in a single row.

The problem

Someone with a pine leaning on the roof isn't going to fill out a form. They call whoever looks local, capable and reachable, usually from a phone and often in a hurry. The site has a few seconds to earn that call.

What we built

A phone-first site. The number sits in the header, in the hero and in a fixed bar on mobile, and there are no contact forms to slow anyone down. Removal, trimming, stump grinding and 24/7 storm work each get their own page, along with one for Sanford. The photos are the crew's own jobs.

How it's put together

Plain, fast static pages. Each service page answers one kind of search instead of cramming everything onto the homepage. Calls from the site are tracked, so the owner can see which pages are producing them.

03 Estimator

What are missed calls costing you?

A quick way to put a number on the calls that ring out. Change the inputs to match your business and the math updates as you type. It's an estimate, not a forecast, and nothing you enter leaves this page.

Your numbers

Example numbers to start from, not industry benchmarks. Replace them with your own.

calls

Calls from people who aren't customers yet. Leave out suppliers, spam and existing clients.

%

Rings out, goes to voicemail or hits a busy signal. Your phone records or call tracking will give you the real figure.

%

They leave a voicemail you return, or call back themselves. The rest have usually rung someone else.

%

Of the new-customer calls you do pick up, roughly how many turn into booked work.

$

In dollars. What a typical job is worth to you, before costs.

The formula, with your numbers

  1. 25 calls a week × 52 ÷ 12108.3 calls a month
  2. 108.3 × 30% unanswered32.5 missed calls
  3. 32.5 × (100% − 25% reached later)24.4 callers lost
  4. 24.4 × 40% would have booked9.8 jobs
  5. 9.8 × $1,200 a job$11,700 a month

What this assumes

  • Missed callers would have booked at the same rate as the ones you answered. In practice some wouldn't, so read this as the upper end.
  • Every lost job is worth your average job value, and you had the capacity to take it.
  • A month is 52 ÷ 12 weeks. Figures are rounded for display.
  • This is an estimate of opportunity. It is not a prediction or a guarantee of revenue.

If you don't know your real missed-call rate, that's the first thing call tracking answers. We're happy to look at it with you, no pitch required.

04 Approach

We don't guess. We measure.

par·al·laxnoun

The way an object seems to shift when you look at it from two different positions. Surveyors and astronomers use that shift to measure distances they can't walk.

We use the same idea. Look at your marketing from the spending side (ads, pages, hours) and from the customer side (calls, quote requests, booked jobs), and you can see how far apart they really are.

  1. 01

    Leads traced to their source

    When the phone rings, you should know whether it was the ad, the map listing or the website. We set up call and form tracking so that question has an answer.

  2. 02

    No vanity metrics

    Traffic and impressions are context, not the goal. If a report can't tell you whether more of the right people called, it isn't doing its job.

  3. 03

    Built for home services

    We work with small, local service businesses: remodelers, tree crews and trades like them. Businesses where a lead is a phone call, the work is seasonal and the service area is a map.

Straight answers

What does it cost?

It depends on what you need, the scope of the work, your ad budget and the size of your business. You get a clear quote before anything starts.

Is ad spend included in your fee?

No. Ad spend is paid by you, directly to Google or whichever ad platform is in use. It's separate from our fees and never passes through us.

Am I locked into a contract?

No. Monthly management renews each billing cycle until you cancel, and you can cancel any time. Work continues through the end of the cycle you've paid for.

Do you guarantee results?

No, and we'd be wary of anyone who does. Results depend on your market, competition, season and budget. What we can promise is that you'll see the numbers, good or bad.

What happens to my accounts if I leave?

Your ad accounts remain yours, and we'll help make sure you keep full access to them. Our privacy policy explains how we handle account access and how to revoke it.
